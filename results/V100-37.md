# V100-37 — 32B dense vs 30B MoE: decode re-measured with 96 GB RAM

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) in a Proxmox VM: 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM, driver 580.178.04._

**Result type.** Negative.

**Question.** Earlier notes said "qwen2.5-32b gives 7 tok/s, offloading to RAM kills speed" and "30B-A3B gives
35 tok/s hybrid", but those runs happened when the VM had 32 GB of RAM filled to the ceiling, so the CPU-resident
weights were fighting the page cache instead of staying resident. With 96 GB, is the wall still the card?

**Environment.**

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| GPU / driver / compute cap | Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB, 16384 MiB / 580.178.04 / 7.0 (sm_70) |
| Engine | llama.cpp `llama-bench` (built for sm_70) |
| Models | qwen2.5-32b-instruct Q4 (18.49 GB, dense) · qwen3-30b-a3b Q4 (17.28 GB, MoE) |
| Host | Proxmox VE VM — 24 vCPU (EPYC 7282), 94 GiB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04.5, kernel 6.8.0-151 |
| Variable | `-ngl` = 99 / 40 / 20 |

**Commands** (verbatim from the run script, one loop over both models):

```bash
BENCH=/srv/media/ai-cache/llama-volta/llama.cpp/build/bin/llama-bench
$BENCH -m /models/qwen2.5-32b-instruct.gguf -p 512 -n 128 -ngl 20 -fa 1 -r 2 -o json
$BENCH -m /models/qwen3-30b-a3b.gguf          -p 512 -n 128 -ngl 20 -fa 1 -r 2 -o json
# same with -ngl 40 and -ngl 99
```

**Results.**

| model | file, GB | `-ngl` | prefill tok/s | decode tok/s | bench wall time, s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen2.5-32b Q4 (dense) | 18.49 | 20 | 253.2 | 3.0 | 94.2 |
| qwen2.5-32b Q4 (dense) | 18.49 | 40 | — | does not load | 2.2 |
| qwen2.5-32b Q4 (dense) | 18.49 | 99 | — | does not load | 44.1 |
| qwen3-30b-a3b Q4 (MoE) | 17.28 | 20 | 439.7 | 24.5 | 18.0 |
| qwen3-30b-a3b Q4 (MoE) | 17.28 | 40 | — | does not load | 2.2 |
| qwen3-30b-a3b Q4 (MoE) | 17.28 | 99 | — | does not load | 39.8 |

**Repeats.** `llama-bench -r 2` — two repetitions per row, averaged by the tool; per-repetition spread was not
recorded, so no error bars are claimed.

**Verdict.** The ceiling is the 16 GB card, not system RAM: 96 GB does not let a dense 32B model run at a usable
speed (3.0 tok/s at 20 layers, refuses to load above that). Among large models only MoE is viable here — the same
20 layers give 24.5 tok/s, eight times the dense model. Practical rule: on 16 GB, load a 30B MoE or stay at 14B
dense; skip dense 30B+ entirely.

**Does not show.** Does not show why `llama-bench` refuses above 20 layers: stderr was not kept, so 'does not load' could be VRAM allocation, a tool-level check or an OOM — the 44.1 s and 2.2 s wall times hint at two different failure points. `-r 2` makes llama-bench average two runs internally and the spread was not recorded, so no error bars are claimed.
