# V100-41 — GPT-OSS-20B MXFP4: 21B MoE in 16 GB

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) inside a Proxmox VM: 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM, driver 580.178.04._

**Question.** gpt-oss-20b — MoE 21B with 3.6B active, weights already in MXFP4 (13.8 GB), context 128k; according to the docs ik_llama.cpp on Titan V (same sm_70) FA for this model goes even on GPU with q8_0-KV cache. If the hypothesis is correct, this is the first sensible "large" LLM on our card.

**Result.** GPT-OSS-20B MXFP4 (13,8 GB) runs on 16 GB but only with expert offloading and FA enabled. llama.cpp master 51831d4, CUDA 12.9, llama-bench pp128/tg32, single run: | `-ncmoe` | FA | prefill | decode | |---|---|---|---| | 0 | off | OOM | - | | 8 | off | 297.8 | **3.04** | | 16 | off | 205.0 | 1.93 | | **4** | **on** | **500.1** | **24.0** | | 6 | on | 396.7 | 19.8 | | 8 | on | 331.6 | 16.9 | | 4 | on + q8 KV | OOM | - | Key point: **without FA, the model barely works** (3 tok/s with the same 8 experts on CPU), while with FA, the same 8 layers give 16.9, and 4 layers - 24 tok/s. This is exactly the opposite of what we saw with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite, where FA reduced speed by 160 times: FA behavior depends on architecture, there's no universal rule "enable or disable", it needs to be measured each time. Live generation with `-ncmoe 4 -fa 1`: 41.8 tok/s, but the model spends tokens on a thinking block (`[Start thinking]`), and the response doesn't reach the code by the time 120 tokens are used. For practice, a budget of 400+ tokens is needed. The numbers are worse than those of the other two candidates for the working loop: Qwen3-Coder-30B provides 75.5 tok/s decode (V100-45), DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite - 183.6 (V100-47). Plus, gpt-oss has a 128k context on paper, but with expert offloading, long context will be even slower.
