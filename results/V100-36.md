# V100-36 — Video without the RAM ceiling: clip length and 720p

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) in a Proxmox VM: 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM, driver 580.178.04._

**Result type.** Mixed.

**Question.** The VM used to run with 32 GB of RAM pinned at 99 % of its limit, and video generation hit a wall
at 12 seconds and 177 s of compute per clip. Part of that wall was latent tensors and offloaded weights living in
system RAM, not the 16 GB of VRAM. With 96 GB and a free page cache, where is the real ceiling?

**Environment.**

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| GPU / driver / compute cap | Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB, 16384 MiB / 580.178.04 / 7.0 (sm_70) |
| Pipeline | Wan 2.2 5B (`/srv/media/ai-cache/models/wan22-5b`), fp16, `enable_model_cpu_offload()` |
| Runtime | PyTorch 2.5.1+cu121, Python 3.12.3 |
| Sampling | 20 steps, guidance 5.0, fixed seed 4242 |
| Host | Proxmox VE VM — 24 vCPU (EPYC 7282), 94 GiB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04.5, kernel 6.8.0-151 |

**Commands** (verbatim from the run script):

```python
vae = AutoencoderKLWan.from_pretrained("/srv/media/ai-cache/models/wan22-5b/vae", torch_dtype=torch.float32)
pipe = WanPipeline.from_pretrained("/srv/media/ai-cache/models/wan22-5b", vae=vae, torch_dtype=torch.float16)
pipe.enable_model_cpu_offload()
pipe(prompt=PROMPT, negative_prompt=NEG, height=h, width=w, num_frames=nf,
     num_inference_steps=20, guidance_scale=5.0,
     generator=torch.Generator("cuda").manual_seed(4242))
```

Peak VRAM from `torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()`, process RSS from `/proc/self/status`, clip length = frames / 16.

**Results.**

| resolution | frames | clip length, s | wall, s | VRAM peak, GB | RSS, GB | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 480p | 97 | 6.1 | 175.8 | 12.68 | 41.58 | ok |
| 480p | 145 | 9.1 | 283.3 | 12.71 | 41.58 | ok |
| 480p | 193 | 12.1 | 415.6 | 13.0 | 41.58 | ok |
| 480p | 241 | 15.1 | 12.0 | — | 41.58 | OOM |
| 480p | 321 | 20.1 | 1.1 | — | 41.58 | OOM |
| 720p (1280x704) | 97 | 6.1 | 394.4 | — | 41.58 | OOM in VAE decode: needed 1.72 GiB, 1.61 free |
| 720p, tiled VAE | 97 | 6.1 | 594.3 | 13.55 | 44.03 | ok, 97 frames received |
| 720p, tiled VAE | 145 | 9.1 | — | — | 44.03 | OOM |

**Repeats.** Single run per configuration; a second 720p attempt without tiling OOM'd immediately (0.0 s) instead of
reaching decode, so the 394.4 s figure is the more informative of the two.

**Verdict.** RAM was never the ceiling: process RSS sat at 41.6 GB across every clip length while VRAM moved
12.7→13.0 GB. The wall is the 16 GB card. Without tricks: 12 s at 480p, or 6 s at 720p with VAE tiling — and tiling
costs ~50 % more time (594 s vs 394 s) plus a decode warning that the returned frames may be malformed, so check the
output rather than trusting the exit code.

**Does not show.** Does not show output quality: tiling raised a decoder warning (`Cannot handle this data type: (1, 1, 3), <f4`), and frames were counted, not inspected. The two 720p attempts are not comparable — the first OOM'd at 0.0 s, a later one reached VAE decode and failed after 394 s — so 720p has no clean single-run number.

**Follow-up.** Queued as V100-62 (inspect the tiled 720p frames) — this card is updated when that run lands, not left as a caveat.
