# V100-145 - Gemma 4 26B-A4B at 4 bits: the 16 GB sweet spot arrives

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) in a VM: driver 580.178.04, CUDA 12.9, 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM._

**Question.** A Russian-language review of nine models across 8, 16 and 24 GB picked Gemma 4 26B-A4B as the
16 GB winner: best coding score among models that fit entirely, at about 170 tok/s on a card with 1008 GB/s
of bandwidth. The V100 has 900 GB/s, so the numbers should transfer. Does the model hold that promise on
Volta, and how does it compare with the Flash-Next MoE already measured here?

**Environment.** V100-SXM2-16GB (sm_70), driver 580.178.04, CUDA 12.9, `llama.cpp` build `build129`
(CUDA sm_70). File `gemma-4-26B_q4_0-it.gguf`, 14 439 363 584 bytes, 25.23 B parameters, official Google
QAT quant tuned for 4 bits (`google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-qat-q4_0-gguf`). MoE with 4B active parameters.

**Commands.**
```
llama-bench -m gemma-4-26B_q4_0-it.gguf -p 256 -n 64 -r 3
llama-server -m gemma-4-26B_q4_0-it.gguf -ngl 99 -c 8192 --host 127.0.0.1 --port 18496 -t 8 --jinja
python3 /root/code_eval.py --base-url http://127.0.0.1:18496 --model gemma4 --tasks 40 \
    --max-tokens 700 --no-think --out /srv/media/night/v100_145.json
```

**Repeats.** `llama-bench -r 3` for prefill and decode; three separate 128-token completion runs for the
server number; MBPP is a single run of 40 tasks.

**Result.** The model fits entirely in VRAM and runs fast: prefill **2 696,26 +/- 94,74 tok/s** at pp256,
decode **124,09 +/- 0,53 tok/s** at tg64, a peak of **15 331 MiB** at 8k context, and the server is ready in
**5 s**. Through the HTTP API the decode lands at **105,1 / 107,5 / 107,3 tok/s** (mean 106,6), and **MBPP
pass@1 is 0,575 (23/40)** at 100,4 tok/s and 0,9 s per task.

Against the Flash-Next MoE measured in V100-130, which needs its experts offloaded to RAM: decode 45,1 tok/s
there versus 124,1 here (bench) and 106,6 (API), and the server starts in 90 s there versus 5 s here.

**Verdict.** Positive. The review's 16 GB recommendation survives the move to Volta: a 25B MoE at 4 bits is
the fastest thing measured on this card so far - 2,4x the Flash-Next MoE on decode and 3,5x on prefill - and
its code quality is at least equal (0,575 against 0,51). The reason is structural, not a lucky quant: the
whole model fits in 15,3 of 16 GB, so nothing is read over PCIe during decode, while Flash-Next spends every
token pulling experts from system RAM.

**Result type.** Positive.

**Does not show.** Quality is not a like-for-like comparison: this run used 40 MBPP tasks and V100-130 used
200, so the two pass@1 values (0,575 and 0,51) overlap within their intervals - the honest statement is "at
least as good", not "better". Russian-language quality was not tested at all, even though the review that
prompted this card tested only Russian; that gap is queued as its own follow-up. Longer contexts than 8k
were not exercised, and 15 331 MiB of a 16 144 MiB card leaves little room for a bigger cache.

**Raw.** `raw/runs/V100-145.json` - bench with standard deviations, three API decode runs, the MBPP summary,
the baseline it is compared against, and the download figures.
