# V100-159 - 19.87 GB Q5_K_M over GGML_RPC: what the second card adds

_A 27B model in Q5_K_M does not fit one 16 GB card: partial offload tops out at 7.43 tok/s. Splitting layers over GGML_RPC to a second, slower card gives full offload - and 2.64x the decode speed._

**Question.** A single V100 cannot hold this quant: `-ngl 99` fails on an allocation of 17,923 MiB, the ceiling is 48 of 65 layers with part of the weights in RAM, and decode is 7.43 tok/s (V100-154). If layer splitting over `GGML_RPC` to a second card lets all 65 layers sit in VRAM, decode should exceed 7.43 tok/s and pass@1 should stay at the 0.60-0.65 level of the one-card partial-offload baseline; if it does not, the second card buys nothing.

**Environment.** Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB (sm_70, 900 GB/s), driver 580.178.04, CUDA 12.9, Ubuntu 24.04, 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM, inside a Proxmox VM. Second card: RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB (sm_89, 288 GB/s, driver 617.14) on a desktop PC, `ggml-rpc-server` b12816 on port 50052, reachable over the home LAN; the link speed was not measured. Client: `llama-server` 1.0.0-dev, build 12359, commit b1b770d90, built with `GGML_RPC=ON`. Model: `qwen38-27b-ud-q5km.gguf`, 19,870,878,624 bytes, context 4096. The PC card also drives a desktop, so ~14.6 GB of its 16 GB were free.

**Commands.** Part B: `llama-server -m /srv/media/ai-cache/downloads/qwen38-27b-ud-q5km.gguf -c 4096 -ngl 999 --host 127.0.0.1 --port 18122 --rpc <pc-in-home-lan>:50052 -ts 0.25,0.75`, then `python3 /root/v148_speed2.py 18122 v100_075 128` for decode. Part A used the same line with `-ts 0.5,0.5`, `0.65,0.35` and `0.75,0.25` on port 18120, plus `python3 /root/code_eval.py --base-url http://127.0.0.1:<port> --model <same gguf> --tasks 40 --no-think --max-tokens 1536 --out <json>`. First value in `-ts` is the RPC side, that is the PC.

**Results.**

| `-ts` (PC / V100) | V100 VRAM | decode tg128, tok/s (n=3) | MBPP pass@1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.75 / 0.25 | 5,821 MiB | 12.4 (12.3-12.4) | 0.65 (26/40) |
| 0.65 / 0.35 | 7,699 MiB | 13.4 (12.9-13.7) | 0.65 (26/40) |
| 0.50 / 0.50 | 10,413 MiB | 16.6 (16.4-16.7) | 0.65 (26/40) |
| 0.35 / 0.65 | 12,953 MiB | 19.4 (19.3-19.5) | not measured |
| 0.25 / 0.75 | 14,841 MiB | **19.6** (19.1-20.3) | not measured |

**Result.** Full offload of all 65 layers works in every split tried: the server loads, answers, and V100 VRAM goes from 5,821 MiB (most layers on the second card) to 14,841 MiB (most layers on V100). Decode rises monotonically as layers move to V100, because the second card has 288 GB/s against 900 GB/s: 12.4 -> 13.4 -> 16.6 -> 19.4 -> 19.6 tok/s. The best split measured, 0.25/0.75, gives 19.6 tok/s at 14,841 MiB of 16,380, which is 2.64x the one-card partial-offload baseline of 7.43 tok/s (V100-154). Quality does not depend on the split: the three configurations where MBPP was run all passed 26 of 40 tasks (pass@1 0.65), above the 0.60 criterion. Each server load took about 55 s; a full 40-task MBPP series took 6 to 8 minutes depending on split.

**Artifacts.** `/root/runs/V100-159/`: `decode_<tag>.json` (five), `mbpp_half_half.json`, `mbpp_v100_heavy.json`, `mbpp_v100_heavy2.json`, `server_<tag>.log` (five), `vram_loaded_<tag>.txt` (five); driver scripts `/root/v159.sh` and `/root/v159b.sh`. There is no `run.log` for this run: both scripts were launched through `systemd-run`, so the journal is in `journalctl -u v159b`.

**Repeats.** Decode n=3 per split, mean and range in the table; MBPP one series of 40 tasks per split, greedy decoding with thinking disabled.

**Does not show.** The ceiling is not found: 0.2/0.8 (about 15.9 GB on V100) was not tried, so 19.6 tok/s is the best of five points, not the maximum of the setup. The LAN between the two machines was never measured, so nothing here separates a network limit from a compute limit; with a faster card on the RPC side the balance point would move. Only one model and one quant were tested, and only MBPP as a quality check, so nothing is known about long context or about how a larger MoE behaves. The second card was shared with a desktop session, which caps its share and adds run-to-run variance that the three decode repeats cannot resolve. Prefill was not measured at all.

**Result type.** Positive

**Verdict.** Splitting a 27B Q5_K_M across both cards over RPC does what a single card cannot - 19.6 tok/s against 7.43 at partial offload, with pass@1 unchanged at 0.65.
