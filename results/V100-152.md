**Result.** On 200 tasks of MBPP, the difference between checkpoints is insignificant: **0.470 (94/200)** for the baseline and **0.495 (99/200)** for Swift-1.5, meaning five tasks out of two hundred with overlapping confidence intervals (0.402-0.539 and 0.427-0.564). Pairwise comparison by tasks: Swift won 7 tasks, lost 2, exact McNemar test p=0.18 - this is noise, not an advantage. The first 40 tasks yielded exactly 24/40 and 25/40, as in V100-148, indicating that the harness is reproducible, but forty tasks is an easier sample: on two hundred, the same model yields 0.47-0.50 instead of 0.60-0.625.

**Does not show.** Only Python and MBPP on 200 tasks, other languages and live projects were not pursued; a difference of five tasks is the upper bound of noise at n=200, significance requires 600-800 tasks; the portion of errors due to truncation at `max_tokens=1536` was not investigated; drop from 0.60 to 0.47 when transitioning from 40 to 200 tasks indicates that forty tasks is an easy selection, but how the model behaves in real life on which set is not tested.
