# V100-89 - colibri: a three-tier expert engine, slower than llama.cpp here

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) in a VM: driver 580.178.04, CUDA 12.9, 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM._

**Question.** colibri keeps hot experts in VRAM, warm ones in RAM and cold ones on NVMe, and its catalog
advertises ~144 tok/s for qwen3-coder-30b on CUDA. Our own best for the same 30B-A3B shape is 75.5 tok/s -
does the tiered engine beat it on a 16 GB card?

**Environment.** V100-SXM2-16GB (sm_70), CUDA 12.9. Engine `JustVugg/colibri` at `e479104`, built with
`CUDA_ARCH=portable-pre-ampere NO_TC=1 ./c/coli build` (exit 0, so the pre-Ampere CUDA path does compile).
Model installed from colibri's own catalog: `qwen3-coder-30b`, 19.4 GiB of safetensors.

**Commands.**
```
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri /root/colibri
CUDA_ARCH=portable-pre-ampere NO_TC=1 ./start-here.sh --yes --model qwen3-coder-30b \
    --backend cuda --ctx 8192 --no-start
./c/coli serve --model /root/colibri-models/qwen3-coder-30b --port 8000
curl /v1/chat/completions  # 128 max tokens, temperature 0, three runs
```

**Repeats.** Three generations of 128 tokens; the first is a cold start (7.5 s of model warm-up) and is
kept in the mean but excluded from the headline number.

**Result.** The engine builds and runs on Volta: the server holds **8 529 MiB**, resident weights load in
5.2 s, and decode lands at **16.2 and 16.6 tok/s** on the two warm runs (mean over three runs 12.9).
That is **4.5x below** our 75.5 tok/s record for the same model shape.

Two blockers worth recording, because they cost most of the time here:
- colibri does not read GGUF at all - it wants `config.json` plus safetensors shards, so the 19 GB
  GGUF we already had could not be used and the model had to be installed from its catalog;
- supported families are frontier MoE architectures (deepseek_v4, glm, lfm3, qwen3-moe); `qwen2` is
  rejected with `unsupported model_type`.

**Verdict.** Negative on this card: the advertised ~144 tok/s assumes a card where most experts stay
resident, while 16 GB forces the cold tier into play and the tiering costs more than llama.cpp's
`-ncmoe` offload. colibri is worth another look on a card with 32 GB or more; on the V100 the record
stands.

**Result type.** Unstated - the result reports measurements without an explicit pass/fail verdict.

**Does not show.** No measurement of the NVMe tier specifically (the model directory sits on SSD, and the
tier hit rates were not instrumented), no prefill numbers, and no quality run - the speed gap decided the
card before quality mattered.

**Raw.** `raw/runs/V100-89.json` - build and serve details, three decode runs, both engine blockers.
