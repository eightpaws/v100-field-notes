# V100-42 — MoE experts in 96 GB RAM: -ncmoe on our map

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) inside a Proxmox VM: 24 vCPU, 94 GiB RAM, driver 580.178.04._

**Question.** llama.cpp can hold expert weights MoE on CPU while keeping attention and router on GPU (`--n-cpu-moe N`, `-ot "exps=CPU"`). We have 96 GB RAM and 16 GB VRAM: MoE with 40–80 GB of weights can provide a decent decode, which dense models cannot offer (V100-37: dense 32B — 3 tok/s).

**Result.** MoE larger than VRAM actually works if you offload part of the experts to RAM. Measured on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B Q4_K_M (17.3 GB against 16 GB VRAM), llama.cpp master 51831d4, CUDA 12.9, guest with 94 GB RAM: | `-ncmoe` | prefill pp128 | decode tg32 | |---|---|---| | 4-6 | OOM, does not load | - | | 8 | 411.9 tok/s | 75.5 tok/s | | 12 | 298.7 | 63.3 | | 16 | 247.6 | 58.8 | | 24 | 181.2 | 45.7 | | 32 (32k context, q8 KV) | 39.5 | 25.2 | Conclusion: the entry threshold is 8 expert layers on CPU, further each step linearly degrades both metrics. At 8 layers we get 75 tok/s decode speed for a model that physically exceeds the card by 1.3 GB - this is the best trade-off among those tested (dense 32B on the same card gave 3 tok/s, see V100-37). Additionally: with compressed KV (`-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0`) at 32k context generation yields 25 tok/s with 16 experts on CPU - slow but works where nothing fits without offloading. Pitfall: `-ctk q8_0` is incompatible with `-fa 0` (context is not created), and `-fa 1` on qwen3moe drops speed. For long context choice: either q8-KV with FA, or fp16-KV without FA.
