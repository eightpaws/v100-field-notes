# V100-79 — llama.cpp v1.0.0 vs build 51831d47: MoE prefill regression, dense unchanged

_Measured on a Tesla V100-SXM2 16 GB (sm_70) inside a Proxmox VM: 24 vCPU, EPYC 7282, 92 GiB RAM available, driver 580.178.04, CUDA 12.9._

**Question.** The first stable tag of llama.cpp (v1.0.0, build `b1b770d`) brought a MoE expert cache in RAM, FP8-KV and MTP into the public API, but reports on Volta showed a prefill regression (issue #32245 on 8×V100: 1446 → 283 tok/s; #32810 on Qwen3.8-27B: 964 → 778 at 5k and 822 → 522 at 16k). Is our older build `51831d47` still the better choice for agent sessions on one 16 GB card?

**Environment.**

| GPU | Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB, cc 7.0, 16 384 MiB |
| Driver / CUDA | 580.178.04 / 12.9 |


Peak VRAM across the series: 15 341 MiB. Wall clock for all four pairs: 4 min 39 s.

**Result.** On the dense 7B the two builds are indistinguishable: every point differs by 0.3 % or less, which is inside the ±stdev. On the 16B MoE (`deepseek2`) prefill collapses with v1.0.0 at every context length - 44.2 % at 2k, 38.2 % at 8k, 31.0 % at 16k - while decode moves by 0.1 %, i.e. the regression is in the compute path for prompt processing, not in the token loop. The stdev of the MoE points is 0.1-1.4 % of the value, so the gap is roughly thirty times the noise at 2k and twenty times at 16k.

**Verdict.** Build `51831d47` stays as the default: the card criterion ("upgrade only if prefill does not drop more than 10 %") fails on MoE by a wide margin, and there is nothing to gain on dense models.

**Result type.** Mixed.

**Does not show.** llama-bench only - synthetic prefill and decode, no server, no quality: pass@1 was not measured on either build, so "v1.0.0 is worse" here means slower, not less accurate. The unified-KV / FA interaction could not be tested at all: tag v1.0.0 has no `--kv-unified` flag in `llama-bench`, `llama-server` or `llama-cli`, so the corresponding item of the original plan is unverifiable rather than negative. The new MoE expert cache was not exercised either; that is card V100-81. Numbers come from one VM and one driver; a different host may shift absolute values, though both builds share the same conditions here.
